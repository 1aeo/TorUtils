Using only public, network-wide CollecTor relay data (hourly
consensuses, directory-authority votes, server descriptors), public
IP-to-AS maps, and exact matching of exit-policy port lists, incl.
rule order, this is the timeline for this thread and hiro's 10-05
thread "Security incident affecting some relay operators".

Terms
- Policy A: the exit-policy summary (consensus "p" line)
    reject 25,110,135,137-139,143,445,465,587,993,995,3389
  i.e. exit to every port except these. Every relay with A lists the
  rules in this order: 25,465,587,110,143,993,995,3389,135,137-139,445.
  Ports A rejects, usual use (clear = readable by the exit):
    mail: 25 SMTP, 110 POP3, 143 IMAP, 587 submission (clear, TLS
      optional); 465 SMTPS, 993 IMAPS, 995 POP3S (encrypted)
    Windows: 135 RPC (clear), 137-139 NetBIOS and 445 SMB (mostly
      clear), 3389 Remote Desktop (encrypted)
- Policy B: the exit-policy summary (consensus "p" line)
    reject 25,119,135-139,445,465,563,587,993,995,1214,
           4661-4666,6346-6429,6699,6881-6999
  i.e. tor's default exit policy plus reject 465,587,993,995. Every
  relay with B lists the rules in this order: 25,465,587,993,995,119,
  135-139,445,563,1214,4661-4666,6346-6429,6699,6881-6999.
  Ports B rejects, usual use:
    mail: 25 SMTP, 587 submission (clear, TLS optional); 465 SMTPS,
      993 IMAPS, 995 POP3S (encrypted). B allows 110 POP3 and 143
      IMAP (clear, TLS optional)
    news: 119 NNTP (clear), 563 NNTPS (encrypted)
    Windows: 135 RPC (clear), 137-139 NetBIOS and 445 SMB (mostly
      clear); 136 has no common use (in tor's default range)
    file sharing (mostly clear): 1214 Kazaa, 4661-4666 eDonkey,
      6346-6429 Gnutella, 6699 WinMX, 6881-6999 BitTorrent
- A vs B: both reject 25,135,137-139,445,465,587,993,995, and both
  rule orders start 25,465,587. Only A rejects 110,143,3389. Only B
  rejects 119,136,563,1214,4661-4666,6346-6429,6699,6881-6999. Both
  allow every other port, e.g. 21 FTP (clear), 22 SSH (encrypted),
  23 Telnet (clear), 53 DNS (clear), 80 HTTP (clear), 443 HTTPS
  (encrypted).
- Affected relay: published >=1 server descriptor whose IPv4 summary
  is exactly A or B. A policy match, not proof of compromise.
- Quetzalcoatl: relays in that family's MyFamily line (362
  fingerprints) or with its ContactInfo.
- Other operators: all operators except Quetzalcoatl, incl. TorDola.
- TorDola: relays TorDola and TorDola1-4 of one operator on AS211720
  (Datashield). TorDola4 is new on 10-04.
- New key: a relay fingerprint seen for the first time.
- Operator: one ContactInfo string; each of the 7 relays without one
  counts as its own operator. A proxy: one person may use several.
- Host: one IPv4 address; physical machines are not visible.
- FranTech: AS53667 (FranTech Solutions, BuyVM).
- Reload: new policy, same tor start time (published minus uptime),
  i.e. config re-read. Restart: new tor start time.
- Listed: in the hourly consensus. Needs at least 5 of the 9
  directory authorities to include the relay in their votes, and at
  least 5 to vote it Running (they can reach it).
- Flagged: BadExit or MiddleOnly in the consensus. A flag needs votes
  from more than half of the authorities that use it (BadExit 3 of 5,
  MiddleOnly 5 of 9). Leaving a relay out of a vote = no flag vote.
  BadExit: clients do not use it as an exit. MiddleOnly: clients use
  it only as a middle relay.
- Usable exit: listed with Exit and without BadExit.
- Dates MM-DD 2026, times UTC, a..b = a to b. hh:mm:ss = descriptor
  published (tor start time in items 5, 13); hh:00 = consensus/vote.

What the switch changed (each relay's A or B vs its own policy just
before 10-01, all ports; "opened" = clients can now exit there to it)
- Quetzalcoatl, 277 relays to B (all from one 79-port accept-list):
  opened 65,235 ports, incl. 22 SSH (encrypted), 23 Telnet (clear),
  110 POP3 and 143 IMAP (clear, TLS optional), 3389 Remote Desktop
  (encrypted), 6667 IRC (clear), 6697 IRC (encrypted); closed only
  563 NNTPS, 993 IMAPS and 995 POP3S (encrypted). For mail reading,
  110/143 (clear, TLS optional) were opened, 993/995 (encrypted)
  closed.
- Other operators, 44 exits to A: opened 22 on 26, 23 on 24, 6667 on
  10; closed mail on both clear and encrypted ports (110/143 on 36,
  993/995 on 37), 3389 on 23.
- Other operators, 21 exits to B: opened 22 on 15, 23 on 10, 3389 on
  10, 110/143 on 8, 6667 on 6; closed 993/995 on 20, 563 on 15. 24
  non-exits to B: opened every port B allows (65,311).
- All 366 affected relays with an earlier policy: opened 22 on 342,
  23 on 335, 6667 on 317, 3389 on 311, 110/143 on 309; closed
  993/995 on 334, 563 on 292. For comparison, at 10-01 14:00 1,120 of
  2,767 unaffected relays with an exit policy allowed 22, and 1,665
  allowed 993/995.
- B's mail mix (110/143 open, 465/587/993/995 closed) was on 3 of
  2,767 unaffected relays with an exit policy at 10-01 14:00; A's
  (all mail closed) on 1,085.
- A tor client only uses exits that allow the destination port, so
  closing 993 does not push a user to 143; it means these relays
  carry no IMAPS/POP3S, while they now carry SSH and Telnet, and
  under B POP3/IMAP on 110/143 (clear, TLS optional).
- Inference: opening 22 fits hiro's report of SSH interception. Why
  993/995 were closed is not visible in the data.

Thread questions
- hiro (non-exits made exits): 24 affected relays were non-exits
  before (0 Quetzalcoatl); all 24 got the Exit flag. Of the other
  353, 342 already had an exit policy and 11 were new keys.
- Toralf (does it boil down to the cleartext sudo password?):
  Unlikely for the whole incident: A or B also appeared on 100
  relays of 58 other operators.
- mpan, in hiro's thread (one shared vulnerability, or node by
  node?): Likely coordinated: A reached 40 relays of 21 operators in
  5 min 24 s, 38 by reload (item 4), so one actor already had access
  to all 40. Whether that access came from one shared hole or from
  earlier break-ins is not in the data; the FranTech rates (Who was
  affected) are the strongest hint of a shared factor.
