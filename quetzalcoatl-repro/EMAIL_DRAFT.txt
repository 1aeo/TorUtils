Using only public, network-wide CollecTor relay data (hourly consensuses, directory-authority votes, server descriptors), public IP-to-AS maps, and exact matching of exit-policy port lists, incl. rule order, this is the timeline for this thread and hiro's 10-05 thread "Security incident affecting some relay operators".

Summary
- From 10-01 14:49 to 10-04 22:21, 377 relays of 59 operators (277 Quetzalcoatl, 100 of 58 other operators) switched to one of two exit policies, A or B below, that no relay had used in any consensus or server descriptor since 2025-01-01; of other operators' relays listed at 10-01 14:00, 71 of 101 on FranTech became affected vs 13 of 9,054 elsewhere.
- Of the 366 affected relays with an earlier policy, the switch opened 22 SSH (encrypted) on 342 and 23 Telnet (clear) on 335 and closed 993 IMAPS and 995 POP3S (encrypted) on 334; B, used by 350 relays incl. all of Quetzalcoatl, keeps 110 POP3 and 143 IMAP (clear, TLS optional) open, a mix that only 3 of 2,767 unaffected relays with an exit policy had at 10-01 14:00.
- No relay with A or B has been in the consensus since 10-05 17:00, so no Tor client can use them now. At 10-09 01:00, 41 relays (9 Quetzalcoatl) still publish A or B but are not in the consensus, as only 2-3 of the 9 authorities include them in their votes (5 needed); they are listed at the end so their operators can check them.

- A tor client only uses exits that allow the destination port, so closing 993 IMAPS (encrypted) does not push a user to 143 IMAP (clear, TLS optional); it means these relays carry no IMAPS or POP3S, while they now carry 22 SSH (encrypted), 23 Telnet (clear) and, under B, 110 POP3 and 143 IMAP (clear, TLS optional).
